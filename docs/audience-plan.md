# Building the audience

Reach 5,000 subscribers in six months by publishing a field note every week
from a real agent fleet, and turning every open-source release into a launch.

## Targets

Subscribers are the number that matters; everything else feeds them. These
are targets to beat, not forecasts.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Positioning

We write for the developer running more agents than they can follow, and for
the lead who has to make that safe.

- **Learning in public.** We run a real fleet and report what works and what
  breaks. No golden answer, no hype.
- **Opinionated.** Every note takes a position.
- **Proof over claims.** Real numbers from our own fleet in every note.
- **Tool-neutral.** Works with whichever harness the reader uses.
- **The frame:** one person's attention is the limit; running agents is a
  people and process problem.

## The weekly field note

One note every Tuesday, 800 to 1,500 words, on our own domain and sent as the
newsletter.

1. **One claim** in the title, a finding not a topic.
2. **The evidence:** a number, a chart or a run record from our fleet.
3. **One artifact a reader can run:** a hook, a layout, a script, a config, a
   tool release.
4. **What we don't know yet**, stated plainly.
5. **A three-minute video** for the notes that show the fleet moving.

Four drafts are banked before launch; an editor on contract reviews every
note; a missed week is announced, never silent.

## Measures and decision points

- **Weekly:** new subscribers, open rate, replies, stars, video views, which
  note brought the most signups.
- **Month 3:** under 750 subscribers or under a 30% open rate: change the
  notes, not the products (fewer opinions, more measurements and runnable
  artifacts).
- **Month 6:** under 2,500 subscribers: the audience thesis is weak; lean on
  consulting and paid pilots, keep writing at a lower cadence.
- **Any time:** a note that brings ten times the usual signups: write three
  more on that topic.

## Guardrails

- **Client work stays unnamed.** Numbers and examples come from our own
  repositories; anything learned on client systems is anonymized and cleared
  first.
- **Employers come first for advisers.** Friends employed elsewhere give
  spoken advice only; nothing published names them until their own rules
  allow it.
- **The cadence is the product.** Four notes banked, an editor, and a public
  note when a week is missed.
- **Honest numbers.** Every measurement says what it cannot show.
